The Engineering Challenge

The Road Behind You Matters.

Two-wheeler riders must continuously divide cognitive attention between oncoming traffic, road hazards, rear-view mirrors, and rapid overtaking maneuvers from behind.

CHALLENGE 01

Limited Rearward Awareness

Vibration, mirror placement, and narrow fields of view can limit continuous awareness of vehicles approaching at high speed in blind angles.

CHALLENGE 02

Visual Warnings Divert Focus

Flashing screens or helmet heads-up displays require focal shifts, pulling critical gaze away from the path ahead during split-second maneuvers.

CHALLENGE 03

Retrofit Accessibility Barrier

Most automotive radar systems are locked into expensive luxury cars. Designing an affordable, compact retrofit system for diverse commuter two-wheelers is an unsolved engineering pursuit.

Tactile Alerts: A Purely Intuitive Sensory Channel

SafeRide explores handlebar haptics as a direct, physical notification method. By utilizing the rider's natural grip interface, directional warnings can be perceived instantaneously without adding visual clutter.

Core Capabilities Under Evaluation

Designed for Touch. Built for Focus.

SafeRide translates environmental sensor data into directional tactile patterns right under your palms.

Directional Vibration

Independent left and right vibration actuators are designed to communicate the side associated with a detected vehicle. If a car approaches on your left, your left grip vibrates.

Rear-Traffic Sensing

The prototype explores rear-facing sensors for detecting vehicles behind the rider. Reliable moving-vehicle discrimination is currently being tested under varying road geometries.

No-Screen Interaction

The intended experience communicates warnings purely through touch, helping minimize the need to look at a display, smart phone, or visual dashboard cluster.

Retrofit-Oriented Design

SafeRide is being designed around a compact electronic controller, ergonomic handlebar sleeves, and clean mounting hardware suitable for diverse motorcycle and scooter geometries.

Independent Left & Right Actuator Enclosures

Integrated vibration actuators within each grip sleeve allow distinct tactile feedback patterns, communicating whether approaching traffic is closing in on the port or starboard side.

LEFT HANDLEBAR

Left Haptic Grip

Dedicated vibration actuator for vehicles detected approaching on the left-side lane or blind zone.

RIGHT HANDLEBAR

Right Haptic Grip

Dedicated vibration actuator for vehicles overtaking on the right side or approaching from starboard angles.

System Architecture

How SafeRide Works.

From rear detection to handlebar haptics in milliseconds. A seamless four-step flow engineered for non-distracting awareness.

Stage A: Tail Mounting

Rear Sensor Placement

Sensors mounted discreetly near the tail light or license assembly monitor the road behind the vehicle, continuously gathering distance and closing-speed cues.

Stage B: Processing Hub

Electronic Processing Unit

A weatherproof micro-controller runs threat-filtering algorithms, discriminating static roadside infrastructure from rapidly approaching automobiles.

STEP 01

Detect

Rear-facing sensors scan incoming traffic trajectories and track closing distances behind the two-wheeler.

STEP 02

Process

The electronic controller calculates approach rate and determines if programmed warning criteria are triggered.

STEP 03

Communicate

The system pulses the corresponding left or right grip vibration actuator with customized tactile cues.

STEP 04

Respond

The rider receives intuitive tactile feedback and checks mirrors and blind spots before executing lane changes.

Milestones & Strategy

Development Roadmap.

Our honest, phase-gated approach to building safe, dependable automotive electronics.

STAGE 01 Active Focus

Functional Prototype

Assemble the primary electronic controller, build dual independent vibration drivers, and benchmark grip actuator response latencies.

STAGE 02

Sensing Experiments

Evaluate radar versus ultrasonic sensor placement on diverse motorcycle tail sections; measure blind-spot detection and false alarm rates.

STAGE 03

Controlled Validation

Test vibration distinguishability while wearing riding gloves; evaluate rain resistance, thermal stability, and chassis vibration damping.

STAGE 04

Product Refinement

Design injection-molded enclosures, pursue ARAI/regulatory compliance pathways, refine tooling costs, and investigate distribution models.

Our Philosophy

Engineering a More Aware Ride.

SafeRide began with a simple engineering question: could a motorcycle communicate approaching traffic through the sense of touch?

We are exploring a haptic warning system that translates sensor information into directional handlebar vibrations. Our approach is to start with a functional prototype, test the limitations honestly, and improve the design based on measurable results.

The goal is not to replace mirrors, rider judgment, or established safety practices. It is to investigate whether tactile alerts can become a useful additional source of information for two-wheeler riders.

Transparent Prototype Disclosure: SafeRide is an independent exploratory concept. We make no claims of verified crash prevention, road-legal certification, or finished consumer availability.
